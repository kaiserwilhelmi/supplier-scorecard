    How the score works

    Each KPI earns 0–100 points on a straight line from its floor (0 points) to its target (100 points), capped at both ends. The composite score is the weighted sum. Calculated from purchase-order data rolled up by month; the change column compares against the same-length period just before.

    Set your own weights: type a weight for each KPI or untick the ones you don't track. Weights must add up to 100%. The scores above update right away, and the template you download includes only the columns for the KPIs you use.

    Tiers: Preferred ≥ 85 · Approved 70–85 · Watch 55–70 · Probation < 55.

    KPI definitions follow the SCOR perfect-order components (on time, in full, perfect condition, accurate documents; APICS, 2017) and the supplier-evaluation literature (Weber et al., 1991; Ho et al., 2010). The default weights, floors and targets are this tool's choices, not published benchmarks. Full definitions and citations are in the template.

    All suppliers and figures are fictional, generated for demonstration. Built with Python (data + scoring) and vanilla JavaScript/SVG.

    Score your own suppliers

    Start from the template: it has the exact columns for the KPIs you use, labeled sample rows, an Instructions tab explaining every field and unit, and the KPI definitions with your weights and sources. Delete the yellow SAMPLE rows, add one row per supplier per month, and drop the file below. The .xlsx remembers your weights.

    · change weights

    Columns (header row required): supplier,category,month,spend,pos plus the columns for each KPI you use: on-time on_time_pos, fill rate lines_ordered,lines_filled, defects units_received,units_defective, invoices invoices,invoices_accurate, price agreed_value (optional: contract, quote or PO price; blank rows are skipped). lead_time_days_total is optional.
    Month as YYYY-MM. Works with .csv, .tsv or Excel (.xlsx) files; in a workbook the sheet named “Data” is read. Everything stays in your browser.
